Under the hood +

State, neighbor, objective

A state is a complete city ordering. Its cost is the closed tour’s Euclidean length. A proposal reverses a segment of the route—a 2-opt move—while keeping the first city anchored.

The acceptance rule

Let Δ = candidate cost − current cost. Improvements and ties are accepted. A worse move is accepted with probability exp(−Δ / T). For example, a cost increase of 10 at temperature 20 has about a 61% chance. At temperature 0, worse moves are always rejected.

Cooling and the best route

After each proposal, T ← T × cooling. The displayed temperature is the one used for that decision. We retain a separate best-so-far route because the current route may get worse. The map shows the accepted state, not a rejected candidate.

A fair baseline

Hill climbing starts from the same tour and uses the same sequence of segment-index proposals and the same budget. It accepts improvements and ties only. Separate seeded random generators preserve reproducibility. Once routes diverge, matching segment indices need not produce matching candidate tours.

Limits and complexity

This finite cooling schedule does not guarantee the global optimum; hill climbing can win individual runs. Compare several seeds before drawing conclusions. With k moves and n cities, computation is O(kn). Saving every route for playback also uses O(kn) space.
