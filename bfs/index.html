What am I seeing? +

Node stores a state, its parent, and its depth. Expand checks its neighbors. Python’s yield gives back one child at a time.

BFS removes the oldest queued node first. New states join the back. The goal is checked before expansion, so the goal itself isn’t counted as expanded here.

BFS finds the fewest edges, not the cheapest weighted route. Try “Cycle” to see why states are marked when queued.
