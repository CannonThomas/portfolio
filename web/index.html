The search problem

A state is a grid coordinate. Actions move one cell horizontally or vertically. Entry costs are positive; the starting cell costs zero.

Why A* works here

f(n) = g(n) + h(n). The queue prioritizes the known cost g plus a remaining-cost estimate h. We use Manhattan distance multiplied by a lower bound on step cost. This heuristic is admissible and consistent for this grid, so removing the goal from the queue gives an optimal-cost path.

The comparison

Uniform-cost search uses the same queue logic with h = 0. Equal priorities use insertion order. Improved paths create new heap entries; stale entries are skipped. Parent pointers reconstruct the route.

What the metrics mean

Expanded counts valid queue removals, including the goal—even though its neighbors are not generated. Queue Lab uses a different convention and excludes the goal. Single-run times include trace recording and exclude validation and playback; they are not a benchmark.

Cost and verification

On this grid, search takes O((V + E) log V) time and O(V + E) space, including the heap and trace. Tests compare results against an independent relaxation algorithm on seeded weighted maps.
